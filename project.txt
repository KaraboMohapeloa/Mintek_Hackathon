\title{
\textbf{An Explainable Probabilistic Decision-Support Model\
for Electric Arc Furnace Process Optimisation}
}

\author{
Proof-of-Concept Study
}

\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}

Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) steelmaking requires operators to continuously
assess the current process state and determine the appropriate next operating
action. These decisions influence tap temperature, electrical energy
consumption, process duration, and final steel quality.

This proof-of-concept (POC) investigates an explainable probabilistic
decision-support approach for EAF operation. Rather than predicting a single
future process value, the proposed model estimates the probability distribution
of possible outcomes under different feasible operating actions. Candidate
actions are then simulated and ranked according to their probability of
achieving the desired tap condition while considering additional energy and
process time.

The objective of the POC is therefore not to replace the furnace operator or
automatically control the EAF, but to provide an interpretable recommendation
of possible next actions together with the associated uncertainty.

\end{abstract}

% =================================================
\section{Introduction}
% =================================================

Electric Arc Furnaces are highly dynamic thermochemical systems in which
electrical energy, chemical energy, oxygen injection, carbon injection,
natural gas, metallic charge, and process timing interact to determine the
state of the furnace and the final condition of the steel.

During a heat, the operator must make decisions based on incomplete and
uncertain information. In particular, the final approach to tapping requires
the operator to determine whether the current process should be maintained,
intensified, chemically adjusted, or brought toward tap.

A conventional predictive model may estimate a future temperature or energy
requirement. However, a prediction alone does not directly answer the
operational question:

\begin{quote}
\textit{Given the current state of the heat, what should be considered as the
next operating action?}
\end{quote}

This POC addresses this problem by combining probabilistic prediction with
action evaluation and ranking.

% =================================================
\section{Problem Statement}
% =================================================

The specific problem addressed in this POC is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{
Given the current state of an EAF heat, identify and rank feasible next
operating actions according to their probability of achieving the desired tap
temperature while minimising expected additional energy consumption and
process time.
}
\end{quote}

The POC focuses on the final approach to tap temperature rather than attempting
to optimise the entire EAF process simultaneously.

The proposed system considers a small action space:

\begin{itemize}
\item maintain current operation;
\item increase electrical power;
\item increase oxygen input;
\item increase carbon input where applicable;
\item prepare for tap.
\end{itemize}

The system does not directly control the furnace. It provides an interpretable
decision-support output for the operator.

% =================================================
\section{Research Objective}
% =================================================

The main objective is to develop a simple probabilistic model capable of
estimating the likely outcome of alternative EAF operating actions and ranking
those actions using process-relevant objectives.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Characterise the current state of an EAF heat using historical
process data.

\item Predict the distribution of future tap temperature rather than only
predicting a single value.

\item Evaluate the predicted outcome under different feasible operating
actions.

\item Quantify the probability of achieving the desired tap condition.

\item Include expected additional energy consumption and process time in
the action evaluation.

\item Produce an explainable ranking of candidate next actions.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Feature Engineering}
% =================================================

A major consideration is that the dataset contains both variables that are
available during the heat and variables that are only known after the heat has
finished.

Using final heat information as an input to a model intended for real-time
decision support would result in data leakage.

Therefore, the POC distinguishes between:

\subsection{Current-State Features}

These represent information available to the operator at the decision point.

Examples include:

\begin{itemize}
\item current or accumulated electrical energy;
\item elapsed heat time;
\item current power;
\item accumulated oxygen;
\item accumulated carbon;
\item accumulated gas;
\item latest available temperature;
\item charge information;
\item number of temperature measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Outcome Variables}

These represent information that becomes known later in the heat and are
therefore used as prediction targets rather than real-time inputs.

Examples include:

\begin{itemize}
\item tap temperature;
\item final energy consumption;
\item final heat duration;
\item final steel chemistry.
\end{itemize}

% =================================================
\section{Heat-State Representation}
% =================================================

Instead of treating each heat as a single static observation, the POC
conceptually represents a heat as a sequence of decision states.

For a heat $h$, the state at time $t$ is represented by:

\begin{equation}
S_t =
[
T_t,
E_t,
P_t,
O_{2,t},
C_t,
G_t,
\tau_t,
Q_t
]
\end{equation}

where:

\begin{itemize}
\item $T_t$ is the latest available temperature;
\item $E_t$ is accumulated electrical energy;
\item $P_t$ represents electrical power;
\item $O_{2,t}$ is accumulated oxygen;
\item $C_t$ is accumulated carbon;
\item $G_t$ is accumulated gas;
\item $\tau_t$ is elapsed process time;
\item $Q_t$ represents relevant charge information.
\end{itemize}

This state representation allows the model to answer a sequential decision
question rather than simply performing heat-level prediction.

% =================================================
\section{Candidate Operating Actions}
% =================================================

For simplicity, the POC uses a small discrete action space:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A} =
{
A_{\mathrm{maintain}},
A_{\mathrm{power}},
A_{\mathrm{O_2}},
A_{\mathrm{carbon}},
A_{\mathrm{tap}}
}
\end{equation}

where each action represents a feasible operational alternative.

The action space is intentionally small so that the output remains
understandable to an operator.

Future implementations may replace these discrete actions with continuous
control variables.

% =================================================
\section{Probabilistic Modelling Methodology}
% =================================================

The central feature of the proposed approach is that the model predicts a
distribution of possible outcomes rather than a single deterministic value.

For example, the tap temperature is represented as:

\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{tap}}
\sim
p(T_{\mathrm{tap}} \mid S_t,A_t)
\end{equation}

where $S_t$ represents the current EAF state and $A_t$ represents a candidate
operating action.

A simple probabilistic regression model may be used for the initial POC.

The model can therefore produce:

\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{tap}}
\mu_T \pm \sigma_T
\end{equation}

rather than only:

\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{tap}} = \mu_T
\end{equation}

This uncertainty is important because EAF operation contains substantial
process variability.

% =================================================
\section{Outcome Prediction}
% =================================================

For each candidate action, the model estimates:

\begin{enumerate}
\item expected tap temperature;
\item uncertainty in tap temperature;
\item probability of achieving the target temperature;
\item expected additional energy;
\item expected additional process time.
\end{enumerate}

The probability of achieving the target condition can be expressed as:

\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{target}}
P(T_{\min} \leq T_{\mathrm{tap}} \leq T_{\max}
\mid S_t,A_t)
\end{equation}

This allows the operator to see both the predicted outcome and the confidence
associated with that outcome.

% =================================================
\section{Optimisation Objective}
% =================================================

The optimisation problem is defined as the selection of the action that
provides the most favourable trade-off between process success, energy
consumption and process time.

The action score is defined as:

\begin{equation}
J(A)
w_T P_{\mathrm{target}}(A)
w_E E_{\mathrm{additional}}(A)
w_t t_{\mathrm{additional}}(A)
\end{equation}

where:

\begin{itemize}
\item $P_{\mathrm{target}}$ is the probability of achieving the desired
tap temperature;
\item $E_{\mathrm{additional}}$ is expected additional energy;
\item $t_{\mathrm{additional}}$ is expected additional process time;
\item $w_T$, $w_E$, and $w_t$ are user-defined weights.
\end{itemize}

The preferred action is therefore:

\begin{equation}
A^*
\arg\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}} J(A)
\end{equation}

The optimisation is performed only over feasible operating actions.

\section{Explainability}
% =================================================

Explainability is a central requirement of the POC.

The system should provide not only the predicted action ranking but also the
main process variables influencing the prediction.

For example:

\begin{quote}
Increasing electrical power is ranked highly because the current temperature
is below the target region and historical process behaviour indicates a high
probability of reaching the target under increased electrical input.
\end{quote}

The model can therefore be interpreted through:

\begin{itemize}
\item current process state;
\item candidate action;
\item predicted outcome;
\item probability of success;
\item expected energy requirement;
\item expected time requirement.
\end{itemize}

This creates a transparent chain:

\begin{equation}
\text{Current State}
\rightarrow
\text{Candidate Action}
\rightarrow
\text{Predicted Distribution}
\rightarrow
\text{Probability of Success}
\rightarrow
\text{Action Ranking}
\end{equation}

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
% =================================================

The POC should be evaluated using both predictive and decision-oriented
metrics.

\subsection{Predictive Performance}

The probabilistic temperature model can be evaluated using:

\begin{itemize}
\item Mean Absolute Error (MAE);
\item Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE);
\item prediction interval coverage;
\item calibration of predicted probabilities.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decision Performance}

The decision-support component can be evaluated using:

\begin{itemize}
\item probability of achieving the target temperature;
\item additional energy required;
\item additional process time;
\item frequency of target-condition violations;
\item comparison with historical operator/process behaviour.
\end{itemize}

The purpose is not simply to minimise prediction error. The model should
provide useful and calibrated information for selecting among feasible next
actions.

% =================================================
\section{Data Leakage and Temporal Validation}
% =================================================

Because the proposed system is intended to support decisions during a heat,
future information must not be used when constructing the current state.

For example, final tap temperature, final energy consumption and final oxygen
consumption must not be used as predictors for an earlier decision point.

The dataset should therefore be divided temporally where possible, with
earlier heats used for training and later heats used for validation/testing.

This better represents the intended deployment scenario.

\section{Innovation}
% =================================================

The innovation of this POC lies in combining three concepts:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Probabilistic prediction} -- uncertainty is explicitly
represented rather than hidden.

\item \textbf{Action simulation} -- the model evaluates possible next
operating actions rather than only predicting the final heat outcome.

\item \textbf{Explainable ranking} -- the system provides an interpretable
comparison of actions based on probability of success, energy and process
time.

\end{enumerate}

The proposed approach therefore moves from:

Prediction

to:

Prediction
+
Uncertainty
+
Action Evaluation
+
Optimisation
.

% =================================================
\section{Scope and Limitations}
% =================================================

The POC intentionally has a limited scope.

The first version focuses on tap-temperature achievement and does not attempt
to perform full EAF control or comprehensive steel-grade optimisation.

The following limitations are recognised:

\begin{itemize}
\item The available dataset is primarily heat-level data.
\item Some variables have substantial missingness.
\item EAF chemistry measurements are not available for every heat.
\item Historical data may not contain explicit operator actions at every
decision point.
\item The model represents statistical relationships and should not be
interpreted as a complete physical EAF model.
\end{itemize}

These limitations motivate the use of a decision-support framework rather than
an autonomous control system.

% =================================================
\section{Future Development}
% =================================================

The POC can subsequently be extended in several directions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Incorporate high-frequency EAF time-series data.

\item Construct real-time heat-state estimation.

\item Include steel chemistry as an additional optimisation constraint.

\item Extend the action space to continuous power, oxygen and carbon
control.

\item Incorporate physical and thermochemical constraints.

\item Develop model-predictive control based on the probabilistic model.

\item Compare the statistical model against physics-based and hybrid
models.

\end{enumerate}

% =================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =================================================

This POC proposes a simple and explainable probabilistic approach to EAF
process optimisation.

The central problem is to determine which feasible operating action should be
considered next given the current state of the heat.

Rather than producing a single deterministic prediction, the model estimates
the probability of achieving the desired tap condition under each candidate
action. These actions are then evaluated using expected energy and process
time and presented as a ranked set of alternatives.

The resulting system is intended to support, rather than replace, the
pyrometallurgy operator. Its principal contribution is the transformation of
historical EAF process data into an interpretable probabilistic decision-support
framework.

The POC can therefore be summarised as:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{Current EAF State}
\rightarrow
\text{Probabilistic Outcomes}
\rightarrow
\text{Action Evaluation}
\rightarrow
\text{Action Ranking}
\rightarrow
\text{Operator Decision}
}
\end{equation}
